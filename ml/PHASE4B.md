## Evidence from the source audit

An eligible record needs authentic English email received from 2023 onward, known collection origin and date or date range, affirmative usage terms for the intended use, and an independently reviewed label. A source's release date does not establish when its mail was sent. For legitimate mail, use verified sender provenance and message context. For phishing, require evidence of a deceptive attempt to obtain credentials, money, access, or data, or to deliver a harmful payload. Exclude generic spam, simulations, generated rewrites and records with unresolved labels. Gmail Spam placement and either SentinelMesh signal are irrelevant to the label. No private mailbox contents were used.

The source review admitted **0 rows** to training, development, or test. No coverage was established for legitimate promotions, newsletters, account notices, receipts, or correspondence at the required source and group diversity. In particular, no rights-cleared modern English release examined here supplies verified receipts and ordinary correspondence at the required counts. Real attack archives are leads, but none contributed a reviewed campaign-separated phishing set. Overlap against a future candidate training set and a future holdout therefore remains **unmeasured**, with no empty-set “zero overlap” claim. No message dataset was downloaded for this phase.

## Frozen protocol for the first eligible comparison

1. Record origin, received date or range, usage terms for training or evaluation, label evidence, category, and review decision for each row in an ignored local intake ledger. Use two reviewers blinded to scores, adjudicate disagreements, and keep unresolved rows `uncertain`. Use one canonical source ID across mirrors and versions. Merge campaign, thread and template relationships into one globally unique connected group ID before assigning a split. A group stays in one split. Prefer later periods for test and reserve whole collection origins where possible. Record any unavoidable shared time range and keep at least one test origin per class out of train and dev.
2. Require after exclusions: **dev at least 250 legitimate and 150 phishing**, with at least 50 per legitimate category and 20 per phishing category. Require **test at least 500 legitimate and 300 phishing**, with at least 100 per legitimate category and 50 per phishing category, at least 50 legitimate groups and 30 phishing campaign groups, and at least three source collections per class. The required legitimate categories are `promotion`, `newsletter`, `account_notice`, `receipt`, and `correspondence`. The required phishing categories are `credential`, `payment`, `malicious_attachment`, and `qr_or_other_link`. Candidate training must have at least two collection origins for each class. These floors reduce dependence on a single source and prevent a tiny slice or a repeated campaign from standing in for the intended coverage. Passing them does not guarantee enough precision for a switch.
3. Use the [Phase 4A JSONL format](PHASE4A.md#run-the-offline-evaluator) and the split audit below. The audit hashes the three inputs, checks IDs and groups across splits, and reuses exact and near text matching. It checks dev and test against the verified v1 Enron/Nazario reference, and checks all role pairs, including the candidate training file against test. Resolve collisions, rerun and freeze the resulting files and manifest. A split auditor can inspect content and labels to remove leakage, but the model developer must keep test rows and predictions out of model selection. The command checks declared fields and content overlap. A reviewer still verifies rights, dates, English language, and completeness of source/group IDs.
4. Once the dataset qualifies, fit one CPU TF-IDF/logistic regression candidate using `subject-body-v1` initially. Select the candidate threshold on dev only: among operating points losing at most **3 percentage points of recall** versus v1, minimize dev FPR, breaking ties by higher recall then lower threshold. Record the frozen candidate hash, threshold, and configuration before scoring test. Use the existing evaluator's scoring and reporting. Its loader currently checks for the v1 bundle version, so a future candidate needs an explicitly supported bundle version that preserves v1 checks. If evaluating the HTML fix later, first use matched synthetic Gmail MIME and offline table cells, then run a development-only ablation holding training data fixed. This phase made no preprocessing change.
5. Score both frozen models on the same test IDs exactly once. Report confusion matrices, false-positive counts and rates, recall and precision by source and category. Treat precision in the assembled corpus as conditional on its class balance. Report failed scoring and missing outputs explicitly. Resample whole groups with both models paired, using 10,000 bootstrap replicates with seed 42. Report percentile 95% intervals for paired FPR improvement and recall difference, and counts with uncertainty for small slices. Keep Spanish evaluation separate.

Switch only when test FPR falls by **at least 2 percentage points and at least 30% relative** to v1, and the lower 95% interval bound for that improvement exceeds zero. The observed phishing recall loss must be **at most 3 percentage points**, with the lower 95% interval bound for candidate-minus-v1 recall at least −3 points. If v1 has zero test false positives, the reduction criterion cannot be met. The FPR targets require a visible reduction in legitimate messages being flagged, while the recall guard limits the loss of useful phishing signals. In addition, any required legitimate category with a point FPR increase of more than 5 percentage points blocks the switch. Insufficient data or inconclusive intervals mean keep v1. Do not retune after opening test. For a future deployment selected via `SENTINELMESH_MODEL_DIR`, point that setting back to the preserved v1 directory, restart, and verify the displayed model version.
